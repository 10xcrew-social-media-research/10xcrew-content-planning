01

Make 10XCREW a familiar name and face

The goal is brand awareness

We want people at relevant companies to recognise 10XCREW and understand the team behind it. Show our people, events, everyday work and collaborations. These channels are not aimed at selling products to consumers.

Linda already has a content plan. I will work with her on ideas, filming, editing and posting. We choose what fits that plan and divide the work together.

What I can help with

  • Research audiences and turn topics into hooks and shot lists.
  • Film, edit and prepare captions, subtitles and covers.
  • Schedule and publish the pieces we agree on.

What we decide together

  • Which ideas fit upcoming work and events.
  • Who makes, reviews and posts each piece.
  • What the results tell us about the next videos.

Colleagues can appear in content or share expertise. Linda and I review the content before publication and agree any client or partner involvement.

Research question and approach

Question: which audiences, stories and openings can make 10XCREW more recognisable through Instagram and YouTube, with LinkedIn supporting the same company story?

This builds on the channel review and audience research already completed. The next step connects the findings to specific content, audience needs, filming choices and tests. Public YouTube listings, company material and 10XCREW's LinkedIn Page analytics were checked on 24 September 2026, alongside official platform guidance. The LinkedIn review covers follower demographics, page visitors and nine posts published between 1 July and 23 September.

Evidence and interpretation: public posts show what was published and the response visible from outside the account. They do not identify everyone who watched. LinkedIn account analytics now add measured professional audience groups and content results. Instagram and YouTube audience choices remain proposals to check in their own account insights. Suggested scripts, timings and posting slots are production ideas.

02

Who we want to reach on each platform

Instagram introduces the crew visually. YouTube gives viewers more time to get to know our people and work. Both should help people connect a face, activity or company collaboration with 10XCREW.

Instagram

Make the crew recognisable.

Primary audience: people in brand, marketing and social commerce teams, including relevant people in our clients' and partners' networks.

Secondary audience: potential colleagues, interns and the wider professional community.

  • What they should see: who works here, what working with us looks like and which brands we work with.
  • Reels: introduce a colleague, show an office moment or follow the crew at an event.
  • Stories and photos: show moments around the video and invite a simple response.
  • Why they might stop: a familiar brand, a real person or an interesting moment in the opening shot.

Use @10xcrew_agency. End with an invitation such as “Follow for more from the crew.”

YouTube

Help viewers understand the team and its work.

Primary audience: marketplace and e-commerce managers, brand teams and business owners who want to understand the people and work behind 10XCREW.

Secondary audience: partners, event contacts and potential colleagues.

  • What they should see: how the crew approaches a project, collaborates with a brand or shares knowledge.
  • Shorts: one person, moment or practical answer that makes sense on its own.
  • Longer videos: a project story, team introduction or event recap when there is enough material.
  • Why they might watch: a clear question, recognisable company or view inside the work.

Use clear titles and familiar faces across videos so viewers can recognise the series.

LinkedIn: reach brand and e-commerce professionals with selected people stories and collaborations. See the sample and page improvements. TikTok stays on hold.

Deeper audience analysis: Instagram

Brand and social commerce teams

What they may want to understand: what kind of agency 10XCREW is, who makes the work and whether the team understands brands like theirs. A familiar company or product can give them a reason to look; the story then needs to make 10XCREW's contribution visible.

Content choice: a collaboration Reel with a colleague on screen, the approved brand name and one concrete task. Try the opening “The crew behind this brand project.” This gives the viewer both a recognisable context and a person to remember. It is a hypothesis to test, not a confirmed preference of our followers.

People in client and partner networks

What connects them to us: the company, event or person they already know. Start a recap with that connection, then show how 10XCREW is involved. Avoid assuming everyone knows the agency before watching.

Distribution: where both sides want it, discuss an Instagram Collab post. An accepted Collab can appear on the participating profiles and reach their audiences. A regular tag alone does not guarantee that distribution. Choose collaborators for the relevance of their network, alongside the size of their following. Meta explains Collabs.

Potential colleagues and the wider crew network

What they may want to see: the people, atmosphere and real working day. Meet the Crew and office Stories fit this question. Show the work as well as the social moment, so viewers associate the content with the company.

How we check the fit: review the available reach breakdown, profile activity and replies with Linda. Note whether comments concern the work, the people or only the joke. Professional audience fit cannot be established from age or total views alone.

Deeper audience analysis: YouTube

A new viewer discovering a Short

They may have no previous connection with 10XCREW. Give them enough context in the opening: a real person, their role and a clear activity or question. “What does our marketplace team do?” is easier to place than an unexplained office montage.

Test: a people introduction and a version built around one work question. Review whether viewers stay to watch, then check whether comments or subscriptions suggest interest in the crew or topic. We cannot identify viewers' job roles from views.

A brand team getting to know the agency

They may arrive through a colleague, an event or a link. A project video can answer “Who would we work with?” and “How does this team approach the work?” Show the people, the task, each person's contribution and one lesson. A clear two- or three-minute story is a starting format to test.

Recognition cue: repeat the series title, keep the colleague's name and role visible, and use a consistent thumbnail. YouTube recommends familiar formats, hosts and branding when developing a returning audience. YouTube audience guidance.

A marketplace or e-commerce professional with a question

They need the topic to be useful before they have a reason to care about the agency. Let a specialist answer a specific question, then connect the explanation to their role at 10XCREW. This keeps expertise in the mix while introducing a recognisable person.

Test: compare discovery through search, the Shorts feed and other sources in Studio. Use the search terms available to refine future titles. A question in the title helps explain the video; it does not guarantee search traffic. YouTube content reports.

03

Connect the opening to the audience

A hook is the opening line or shot. Put the person, activity or question on screen immediately, then deliver what the opening promises.

A face behind the work

“Who is behind the work at 10XCREW?”

Instagram Reel. For brand teams and potential colleagues: introduce a person and show their role in action.

A view inside the office

“Come behind the scenes with the crew.”

Instagram Reel or Stories. For people curious about the company: start with the team doing something together.

A recognisable collaboration

“What does working together on a brand project look like?”

YouTube or LinkedIn. For brand teams and partner networks: show the agreed company name and a real moment from the work.

A moment from an event

“One moment we wanted to share from today.”

Instagram Reel or YouTube Short. For event contacts and the wider network: open with that moment, then show who was there.

A question for an expert

“What do you check first when a brand joins a new marketplace?”

YouTube Short. For marketplace and e-commerce teams: show a named colleague giving one clear answer.

An introduction to the team

“What happens before a brand project goes live?”

Longer YouTube video. For people getting to know the agency: follow the people involved and explain each step simply.

How we test the hooks and attract the right people

People test: compare “Meet the crew” with “What does our marketplace team do?” across comparable introductions. The first emphasises the person; the second adds a work cue for brand and marketplace teams.

Collaboration test: compare an opening built around the approved company name with one built around the shared task. The company may attract people who recognise it; the task may attract people working on a similar problem. Both need to identify 10XCREW's role.

Event test: compare a venue opening with a colleague sharing a memorable moment. The colleague version gives people a face to connect to the event. Keep the event name visible for contacts who know it.

Fair comparison: keep platform, topic, length and measurement period as similar as practical. Try each approach on more than one comparable post. Ordinary organic posts are not a controlled experiment, so treat differences as clues. Review watch time or retention together with relevant comments and profile interest, then repeat the most promising approach.

The opening should deliver a real story. Avoid a dramatic promise that the footage cannot support, or a long logo animation before viewers see the people.

07

Company branding means showing the company

People should gradually recognise the crew, our way of working and the look of 10XCREW. The branding comes from the stories we show as well as the design.

Keep returning to familiar formats

  • Meet the Crew: faces, names and roles.
  • Crew at Work: everyday teamwork and brand projects.
  • Crew on Location: real events, visits and shared moments.
  • One Question: an expert explains one useful point.

Make each video look like 10XCREW

Use existing templates, colours and typography. Put real people or activity in the opening shot, with a readable title and consistent subtitles.

  • Petrol
  • Teal
  • Bright teal
  • Orange
  • White

Repeat the series name and cover layout. Keep the language natural and let the people speak in their own words.

How we adapt the GoSpooky inspiration

GoSpooky's culture page puts the team and office atmosphere forward. Its event format brings brands and people together around a topic.

Our adaptation is to use that emphasis on people and shared moments for 10XCREW: our own crew, existing events and real collaborations, filmed in our style. Use original footage, wording and 10XCREW templates. A new event or series only enters the calendar if Linda wants to develop it.

Translate the inspiration into recognisable 10XCREW content

People and culture: the GoSpooky reference makes its people and atmosphere part of its company story. For 10XCREW, build a Meet the Crew series around actual roles, with the same name treatment and cover style each time.

Events: use an event already in the calendar to show the people meeting, contributing and learning. Make the event name and 10XCREW's involvement clear. The useful lesson is a repeatable story format, not a requirement to organise another event.

Collaborations: give the company we work with a visible place in the story, and explain our role. A logo without context leaves viewers to guess what the relationship is.

Visual brief: real footage first; petrol and teal for recurring titles and covers; readable subtitles; a consistent place for names and roles; a short, recognisable ending. Review a few posts together as a group to see whether they clearly belong to the same company.

This is our adaptation of the references linked above. Their public presentation does not prove that the same approach will produce the same audience response for 10XCREW.

Measure visibility, audience fit and recognition separately

Visibility: did people have a chance to see us?

Use reach or unique viewers where available, alongside views. Compare each platform against its own starting point. Repeated plays and different platform definitions mean the counts should not be added into one audience total.

Audience fit: does the response connect to our work?

Review relevant comments and replies, interest in the people or projects, and available audience information. On LinkedIn, use professional demographic groups. On Instagram and YouTube, job roles are not established by aggregate viewer counts.

Recognition: do they connect the story to 10XCREW?

With Linda, note real examples of contacts recognising a colleague, recalling a video or mentioning a collaboration. A small optional follow-up with business contacts can ask “Which 10XCREW content do you remember seeing?” and “What did it tell you about the company?” Record answers as qualitative feedback, not a representative awareness score.

Compare the same kind of result

Review the next 30 complete days against the LinkedIn baseline, recording posting volume and any paid support too. More posts can increase total impressions without improving each post. Keep the seven-day post snapshots separate from these account totals. This plan uses awareness measures rather than consumer sales targets.

What to change after a review

If people leave early, test a clearer opening. If a video gets views but little connection to the work, make the role or collaboration more visible. If relevant people respond, develop another story in that format. Keep useful formats long enough to learn from several posts, and discuss the next choice with Linda.
