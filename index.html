02

What fits each channel

Each channel has a different purpose. These are the audiences we want to reach and the content we can make for them.

LinkedIn

Show our knowledge and experience.

For marketplace managers, e-commerce leaders, brand teams and founders.

  • Practical tips and clear answers to common questions.
  • Client cases with a supported result and its measurement period.
  • Crew updates and useful lessons from events.

Give readers the main point in the post itself. Publish through the company page using Linda's schedule.

Instagram

Show the people behind 10XCREW.

For potential colleagues and brand teams who want to get to know us.

  • Meet the Crew and short office Reels.
  • Stories from events and training days.
  • Simple tips from the team, explained visually.

Use @10xcrew_agency. Reuse footage from the same shoots where it fits.

YouTube

Make useful expertise easy to find.

For people looking for marketplace answers or a talk on a specific topic.

  • Shorts that answer one question.
  • Clips from existing talks and interviews.
  • Longer explanations when the subject needs them.

Use a clear title that says what viewers will learn. Agree video dates within the existing planning.

TikTok stays on hold. We can review the audience assumptions using the accounts' analytics as we work.

03

Give each post a clear opening

A hook is the first line or opening shot. It should make the topic clear and give the right person a reason to keep reading or watching.

A practical question

“bol or Amazon first for a Dutch brand?”

For marketplace managers and founders. Follow with one useful answer.

A result from our work

Open with one supported result from a client case, then explain what changed.

For e-commerce leaders and brand teams. Include the source and period.

A person and their expertise

Introduce a colleague through a question they help clients solve.

For potential clients and colleagues. Show what their work involves.

Inside a working day

“A day with Pacvue at our Eindhoven office.”

For people interested in the crew, our partners and how we work.

A timely change

Explain what an upcoming platform update or event means for a brand.

For people responsible for that platform. Check the date before posting.

One question, one minute

Use a short video to explain one practical point from the team's knowledge.

For LinkedIn, Reels or Shorts. Keep the answer focused.

04

Three examples we can develop

These give us something concrete to discuss. We can adapt them to the content and dates already in Linda's planning.

LinkedIn · Company-page post

Explain a retail media comparison

A click on Albert Heijn's retail media cost 98 cents on average. On Amazon it was 29 cents.

Example opening based on 10XCREW's published Retail Media Day 2025 comparison.

Use the comparison to explain one useful takeaway in plain language. Add a short video or a simple visual, with the source and measurement period.

We develop the post together and ask a specialist to check the explanation if needed.

Instagram · Reel

A day with Pacvue

A Wednesday in August at our Eindhoven office. The Pacvue team spent the day with our performance team.

Example caption for footage from the August 2026 training day.

Make a short Reel from available footage: the office, the training, the crew and a closing thank-you to Pacvue.

Aim for about 30 seconds in a vertical format. Choose the clips and captions with Linda and confirm the footage can be used.

YouTube · Short

What an Amazon audience test can teach us

Amazon ads: what can we learn from testing a different retargeting audience?

Suggested title, based on the existing 10XCREW case about Amazon Marketing Cloud.

Explain the question, what was tested and what the case found. Finish with one lesson for a marketplace manager.

Use existing material where possible. Keep it around 45 seconds, with subtitles and a clear source for any result shown.

05

Build on Linda's existing planning

The topics, dates and posting frequency come from the existing plan. We agree where I can help and how to divide each task.

  1. Check the plan

    Look at upcoming posts together. Choose what needs help and agree who prepares, reviews and publishes it.

  2. Prepare content

    Divide the writing, visuals and video work. Gather any facts, footage or input we need from colleagues.

  3. Review together

    Check the message, facts, spelling and branding. Make the changes and get Linda's approval.

  4. Publish

    The agreed person schedules or posts the content on the right 10XCREW account.

  5. Learn from it

    Review reactions, views, comments and relevant clicks together. Use what we learn for the next posts.

07

Show our people and keep the branding consistent

Formats we can reuse

  • Meet the Crew: introduce a colleague and their work.
  • Crew at Work: show how the team approaches a task.
  • Event Days and Crew on Location: share useful moments from events.
  • Welcome to the Crew: introduce new colleagues.
  • Crew Moments: share a natural, light moment from the office.

Keep the look familiar

Use the existing 10XCREW templates, colours and typography. Keep text easy to read and use real people and places.

  • Petrol
  • Teal
  • Bright teal
  • Orange
  • White

Use vertical clips for Reels and Shorts, add subtitles and agree permission with everyone shown.

From the GoSpooky research: people stories, team life and event content are useful formats to adapt to 10XCREW. A small 10XCREW Huddle event remains an idea to discuss with Linda before adding it to the planning.

08

LinkedIn improvements to work on together

Alongside the posts, we can use the earlier page review to agree which improvements are worth making.

  • Clear company information: check that the tagline and About text explain the same services in English and Dutch.
  • Useful posts: choose content to highlight and repost relevant crew posts when they fit the planning.
  • Consistent profiles: use the correct company name, a clear role and the 10XCREW banner.
  • A starting point for results: save the available analytics before changes and agree what to review with Linda.

For settings we cannot change with our account access, involve the page admin.

Research context behind these ideas

The channel review measured 3,387 LinkedIn followers and a median of 6 reactions per company post on 17 September 2026. The comparison with similar agencies was 15 reactions. These are dated research figures; current account analytics will help us judge the posts we make together.
